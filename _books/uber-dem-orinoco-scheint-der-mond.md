Everything is interconnected, and we humans are part of the whole of nature. This insight has been forgotten in our technology- and economy-driven world, with increasingly negative consequences for our natural environment, our means of subsistence, and ultimately ourselves. How do we find our way out of this? Harald Lesch and Klaus Kamphausen propose a worldview and conception of humanity that once again sees humans as part of natural interconnections and portrays them as beings who only unfold their full, sustainable potential through cooperation and solidarity—a worldview and conception of humanity that moves away from the over-rationalization and over-economization of life and makes more room for wonder and empathy. A guiding light for their reflections is the naturalist Alexander von Humboldt, who traveled the South American Orinoco River over 200 years ago.
